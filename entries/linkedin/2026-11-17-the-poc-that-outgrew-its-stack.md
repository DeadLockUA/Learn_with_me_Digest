I picked my programming language because it was fast for one person on a small game project. Two months later that small project was about 260,000 lines of code, most of it written by AI coding agents. I threw the code away and started from an empty repository.

Nobody decided it was a big project. It grew quietly, from one playable slice to several modes, AI opponents, a strategy layer and save and load. 𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝘀𝘁𝗮𝗰𝗸 𝘄𝗮𝘀 𝘀𝘁𝗶𝗹𝗹 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗼𝗻𝗲 𝗜 𝗽𝗶𝗰𝗸𝗲𝗱 𝗳𝗼𝗿 𝗮 𝗽𝗿𝗼𝗼𝗳 𝗼𝗳 𝗰𝗼𝗻𝗰𝗲𝗽𝘁.

Then it stopped holding. Every map was built on one file of about 5,000 lines. One spec grew so long that the agent rewriting it hit its output limit and was cut off mid-file.

My lesson: 𝗣𝗶𝗰𝗸 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝘀𝘁𝗮𝗰𝗸 𝗳𝗼𝗿 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝘀𝗶𝘇𝗲 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗽𝗿𝗼𝗷𝗲𝗰𝘁 𝘄𝗶𝗹𝗹 𝗿𝗲𝗮𝗰𝗵, not the size of the proof of concept. And make the step from proof of concept to product a decision, not a drift.

Remarkably, just as I was writing this post, a very old and good friend of mine, Kostiantyn Pelikhovskyi, raised the identical problem in his TeleTone case study, from the side where he did everything right.

This is my hobby, so it cost me only a restart and two months of a Claude subscription. For a client, the architecture has to be worked out properly at the very conception of the project. You do not build a Burj Khalifa on a foundation laid for a five-storey house.
