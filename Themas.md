# Themas.md

Raw, unrefined topic ideas — captured here as they come up, to be developed
into an actual entry (topic + connections to other entries, AGENTS.md §2)
later. Not commitments, not drafts; just a scratch backlog so ideas don't get
lost between sessions. Remove an idea from here once it's been turned into a
real entry.

## Ideas

1. **Prototype vs. prompt engineering** — building a working prototype is
   often more productive than exhausting yourself crafting the perfect
   prompt.

2. **Agile principles → AI principles** — take the Agile Manifesto's
   principles and transform them into a set of AI-native principles (see
   idea 1 as a worked example of that transformation).

3. **Quality per token** — one-shot, one-agent solutions are fast but risky;
   there's a quality/cost tradeoff worth naming explicitly.

4. **Autonomous AI work is Waterfall, not Agile** — unsupervised/autonomous AI
   execution behaves like Waterfall: no feedback loop mid-run, so you must
   nail down requirements/validation criteria upfront like Waterfall demands.
   Risk to call out: without a proper validation phase, you don't get "not
   what you wanted" — you get exactly what you asked for, which can still be
   wrong. Connects to idea 2 (Agile→AI principles, as a contrast/counterpoint)
   and idea 3 (quality/cost tradeoff — missing validation is a cost cut).

5. **AI needs to be taught to doubt** — models answer with the same
   confidence whether they know or are guessing, so the burden of doubt
   falls entirely on the human. Worth naming: what "teaching doubt" would
   actually look like in practice — asking back instead of assuming,
   flagging an unverifiable claim, refusing to fill a gap silently — and
   whether that can be instructed at all or only designed around. Connects
   to idea 4 (missing validation phase — doubt is the in-run feedback loop
   autonomous execution lacks) and idea 3 (quality per token — a model that
   stops to doubt costs more turns).
